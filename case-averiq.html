Owning the mobile surface at a remote US startup

{{co_d}} builds enterprise solutions across inventory management, construction and fleet management — the exact intersection of domains the rest of this site is about. I own the mobile layer, in .NET MAUI, from a fully remote seat with {{overlap}} of daily overlap with the US team.

Deliberately short. I started here in {{co_d_dates}}. Writing this up as a deep architectural case study would be dishonest — shipped architecture takes longer than this to have earned an opinion about. What follows is scope and ownership. The Hilti case study is where my depth actually is, and it is where I would rather be evaluated.

Scope

What I own

  • The mobile architecture — persistence and sync model, module boundaries, threading contract, and the API contract negotiated with the backend team.
  • Delivery — build, signing, distribution and release gating, so shipping is repeatable rather than heroic.
  • The technical standard — review expectations, reference implementations, and the architectural guardrails that keep a young codebase from calcifying badly.
Domain

Why this fits

Inventory management, construction and fleet are the same class of problem I have been solving since Gallo Gateway: field users, ruggedised or shared devices, unreliable connectivity, and data whose correctness has commercial consequence. The specific product is new; the failure modes are not.

It is also the first time I have owned the mobile surface with no engineer above me on it, which is the mode I want to keep working in.

Stack

.NET MAUI, continuing the arc

Xamarin.iOS at Gallo, Xamarin.Forms at SmartSite, .NET MAUI at Hilti and now here. Six years on one platform lineage means I have carried real applications through two framework migrations and know where the abstractions leak — which is a different kind of knowledge from having used the current version for a while.
