Screening shortcuts

Questions you were going to ask anyway

Will you write code, or just architect?

Write code. I am an IC by preference and by design — architecture without implementation degrades into slideware, and I want to be the person who writes the reference implementation that everyone else copies.

How do you handle a stack you haven't used?

The transferable knowledge sits below the framework — concurrency, memory, persistence, platform lifecycle. I carried real apps through Xamarin.iOS → Xamarin.Forms → .NET MAUI and picked up Kotlin/Compose and Swift/SwiftUI by mapping them onto that foundation. Ask me to reason about an unfamiliar framework in the interview; it's a fair test and I'd rather take it than pad a skills list.

Greenfield or existing codebase?

Mostly existing, and I'm good at it — retrofitting offline durability into a live product at Siemens Gamesa, migrating authentication on a daily-use app at Gallo, carrying codebases through two framework generations. Rescue and evolution work is where a senior hire usually pays for itself fastest.

What about mentorship and team leadership?

Yes, as a senior IC — review standards, reference implementations, architectural guardrails, onboarding paths. I have scaled junior and intern output this way. What I am not looking for is a people-management track.

Can you work with a codebase you disagree with?

Yes. Inherited decisions usually had reasons, and the first job is to find them. I document the disagreement, propose an incremental path, and keep delivery running while it happens — see the incremental Compose/SwiftUI migration in Era 03.

What's your interview preference?

Architecture and system design discussion, code review of a real pull request, or a scoped take-home I can walk you through. All three tell you far more than an algorithm puzzle about how I'd behave on your codebase.
