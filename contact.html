Common questions

Questions I usually get asked

Will you write code, or just architect?

I write code. I'm an individual contributor because I want to be. Architecture that nobody implements turns into slides, and I want to be the one who writes the reference code everyone else copies.

How do you handle a stack you haven't used?

Most of what carries over sits below the framework: concurrency, memory, storage and the platform lifecycle. I took real apps from Xamarin.iOS to Xamarin.Forms to .NET MAUI, and I learned Kotlin with Compose and Swift with SwiftUI by relating them to what I already knew. Feel free to ask me to reason through an unfamiliar framework in the interview. It's a fair test, and I'd rather take it than pad my skills list.

Greenfield or existing codebase?

Mostly existing codebases, and I'm good at that. I've added reliable offline saving to a live app at Siemens Gamesa, moved sign-in for an app people used every day at Gallo, and taken codebases through two framework generations. Fixing and evolving an existing app is usually where a senior hire pays off fastest.

What about mentorship and team leadership?

Yes, as a senior IC. I do it through review standards, reference code, architecture rules and onboarding guides, and that's how I've helped junior engineers and interns get more done. I'm not looking to become a people manager.

Can you work with a codebase you disagree with?

Yes. Decisions I inherit usually had a reason, and my first job is to find it. Then I write down where I disagree, suggest a way to change it step by step, and keep shipping while that happens.

What's your interview preference?

A conversation about architecture and system design, a review of a real pull request, or a small take-home that I walk you through afterwards. Any of these will tell you more about how I'd work on your codebase than an algorithm puzzle.
